# Research Agenda

ITF 0.1 is a conceptual framework. The following are **hypotheses**, not established findings.

## H1 — Transfer hypothesis

Practitioners explicitly trained to identify valid transferable structure will require less target-specific instruction when learning related unfamiliar technologies than practitioners using implementation-first study alone.

## H2 — Delta hypothesis

Explicitly separating transferred knowledge from genuinely new knowledge will reduce redundant study without reducing task accuracy.

## H3 — Boundary hypothesis

Requiring learners to state transformation boundaries and analogy breaks will reduce negative transfer compared with structural similarity mapping alone.

## H4 — Retention hypothesis

Knowledge organized around lower-volatility structures will remain transferable across more generations of related implementations than product-specific procedural knowledge.

## H5 — Expertise interaction hypothesis

The benefit and risk of ITF will differ by expertise level: novices may lack enough prior structure to transfer, while experts may be more vulnerable to overconfident analogy.

## Suggested experiment design

A minimal controlled study could:

1. teach two groups the same source technology;
2. introduce an unfamiliar but structurally related target technology;
3. give one group a DELTA worksheet and the other a conventional tutorial workflow;
4. measure time-to-correct-mental-model, implementation accuracy, debugging accuracy, retention, and negative-transfer errors;
5. repeat with a target containing a deliberate analogy break.

## Metrics not yet standardized

Terms such as *Cognitive Compression Ratio*, *Knowledge Half-Life*, or *Transfer Coefficient* may be interesting future constructs, but ITF 0.1 intentionally does not define them because no validated operationalization currently exists in this project.

## Evidence standard

Future empirical claims should include:

- preregistered hypotheses when practical;
- reproducible materials;
- explicit participant expertise;
- target-system similarity controls;
- measures of negative transfer;
- effect sizes and uncertainty, not only significance claims.
